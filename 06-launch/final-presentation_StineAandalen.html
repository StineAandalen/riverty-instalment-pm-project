Slide 5 · Strategy

Problem, value and hypothesis

Problem hook

We must solve the rising cost by addressing the user's uncertainties & possibly panic situation.

Value proposition

We will offer a guided portal journey. We are losing most starters today right at the beginning of the user journey, to align our product priorities with our north-star strategic goal of 80% digital self-service Europe-wide.

Data-backed hypothesis

The user enters a super transparent portal. She would easily understand the documents that are requested, and she can easily understand how she can set up a plan that combines several cases and is affordable for her.

Measured by a 5% change in the uplift of the current SSR within 4 weeks, while protecting 60-day plan survival: no more than 19% of new agreements missing a payment within 60 days.

31%of starters contact an agent within 7 days
28%of uploaded income documents need review
19%of new agreements miss a payment within 60 days
If we do nothing for 12 months

The agent costs will increase and we will fall behind the goal of more self-service. The goal of the SSR is 80%.

Risk level

It's a high-stake risk because it touches cost (agent capacity) and strategic portfolio value (company plans that might fail).

Scope of the problem

This is a systemic problem.

PRD highlights: “Why we ask” explanations

The cheapest direct answer to why her income is requested, mostly copy and design plus legal work.

  • Full inventory, then coverage. List every income, affordability and document-upload question in the German portal journey, and give each one a note.
  • A short note on each question (about two sentences) saying why it's needed and how the answer is used.
  • A “who can see it” line in each note, using wording confirmed by data protection.
  • Visible by default, inline, on mobile. The note sits next to the question with no tap required.
  • Legal and data-protection sign-off on the final wording before release.

Demoted from Must: fixed, plain German copy, written by the team and reviewed, not generated at runtime. It is not a must-have and can be done by the content manager or AI.

What the PRD makes explicit

The PRD treats legal-approved wording as a release gate, because a wrong statement about data use would damage the trust it is meant to build, and a vague brief would have left that risk to be discovered in week 3.

Slide 8 · Validation

Experiment plan

Hypothesis

I believe that Why we ask for consumers will result in the user entering a super transparent portal. She would easily understand the documents that are requested, and she can easily understand how she can set up a plan that combines several cases and is affordable for her. The European rollout gets proof: a pilot with evidence, guardrails and a clear stop condition makes extending to other markets and portfolios a decision backed by data, not a leap. SSR becomes a trustworthy north star: reading it alongside the 60-day miss rate means a rising SSR reflects better outcomes.

Measured by a 5% change in the uplift of the current SSR within 4 weeks. We protect 60-day plan survival throughout the test.

Control (A)

31% of starters contact an agent within 7 days, roughly 2,600. The research themes say consumers don't know what's affordable, don't understand why income is requested, and fear the consequences of a wrong answer or missed payment. 28% of uploaded income documents need clarification or review.

Variant (B): exactly 3 screens
  • Start instalment request: “Arrange an instalment plan”, with the intro “We ask a few short questions. Under each one, we explain why.”
  • About your income: monthly net income, income pattern and proof of income (required, no skip option), each with a “Why we ask” note: why asked, how used, who can see it.
  • Confirmation: “Thanks, we have your income details”, a recap with Edit links and the next step.

Held constant (isolation check): consumers still do not trust the “Why we ask” statements and give up and call an agent.

Primary metric
SSR uplift
The instalment is created in the portal with no agent contact 30 days before or after set-up, and the consumer keeps it with no missing payments within 60 days.
Baseline
31%
Journey starters contacting an agent within 7 days
MDE
5%
Sample 2,796 per arm. Split 50/50. Significance 0.05. Duration 4 weeks.
Guardrail
≥ 81%
60-day plan survival: no more than 19% of new agreements missing a payment within 60 days. No room below that baseline. Second guardrail: not set.
Shipping criteria
ShipSSR uplift improves by ≥ 5% at 0.05 and 60-day plan survival does not fall below 81% after 4 weeks.
IterateThe direction is positive but the lift is below the MDE.
KillThe primary metric shows no improvement or moves negatively.

The read date is fixed at the end of 4 weeks. No results are reviewed before this date. Findings and decision: to be added after the experiment runs.

Slide 9 · Launch

Go-to-market and success metrics

Goal

Engagement. We want to encourage users to add the information we ask for.

Launch tier

S, Minimal. We are only adjusting content in an already existing feature.

Feature

Why we ask: more transparent information to justify why we ask for the income details.

Audience

Mara, 38, a shift worker with variable but stable income and two open claims. She can't pay the balance in one go and opens the portal on her phone, after a digital reminder, with very little time. She represents the pilot segment in Germany: consumers who need an instalment plan across single or multiple claims.

Channels (owned) Own consumer portalEmail link to the portalLetter
Enablement and assets

A short demo of how to set up an instalment that matches your income, or add info in any of the existing FAQ.

Ownership and budget

The product manager of this PRD, one person. Build in the portal: one engineering lead, one person. A one-pager for the call: one person in collections who briefs Daniel. The case study names no individuals.

No media spend. The extra cost is the briefing and the measurement of the call within 7 days and the keep rate at 60 days. Do not build: paid campaigns, influencers, a model, an image check, reminders, or a launch outside the Germany pilot.

Timeline
Phase 1, beta50/50 in the one segment, 14 days of enrollment plus 7 days of follow-up. Do not ship on day 21.
Phase 2, launchOnly once, 60 days after agreement, the keep rate in the variant is at least 81% and the call rate has fallen by at least 5 percentage points below 31%. Then in the portal only, for this segment.
Phase 3, after launchKeep reading the keep rate, with the one-pager in use. No further market while the 81% holds.
Success metrics (targets, not results)
31%
Call rate within 7 days today. Target: at least 5 percentage points lower.
≥ 81%
Keep rate at 60 days (19% or fewer missing a payment).
Bad signal to watch for

Many people see the instalment, and the call rate stays at 31%. Or calls fall and the 60-day miss rate rises. Then she agreed without being able to keep the instalment.

Likely post-launch decision

Repeat if the call rate falls by less than 5 percentage points, or it falls and the keep rate is below 81%. Double only if both are true: at least 5 percentage points fewer calls and a keep rate of at least 81%. Lower the priority if the call rate does not fall or it rises.
